# Proposal: `v1-spine`

**Status:** agreed in principle by Prateek, 12 November 2026. Bayesian framing added at his request, 13 November 2026. To be checked against the best open-source bots before building.
**In short:** One idea runs the whole bot. Start from the best prior available, update only on real evidence, then check the updates against reality. Every step is tested against the plain template, and kept only if it helps.

## The four steps
See `ARCHITECTURE.md` for each step in full.
1. **Read the question.** What counts as YES, the source, the deadline.
2. **Prior.** Market price, if the market is liquid and its rules match the question. Otherwise, for numeric questions, a statistical model of the data. Otherwise, a base rate scaled to the time left.
3. **Update.** Five forecasters from different model families. Move from the prior only for real evidence. Combine on the odds scale.
4. **Correct.** Fix measured biases with a Bayesian calibration. Cap at 1% and 99%.

## Why this shape
- Most bots lose points by misreading the question, or by drifting towards the dramatic outcome. Steps 1 and 2 guard against both.
- Liquid market prices and data are usually better priors than a model's guess. Thin markets, markets with different rules, and long-shot prices are not trusted as they stand (Prateek, 13 November 2026). Copying markets only keeps pace with other bots. The edge is in knowing when a market is wrong.
- Most winners correct their bot's raw numbers afterwards (FutureEval bot-maker survey, Fall 2025).
- Combining forecasts on the odds scale, with slight extremising, has beaten the median in research on combining forecasters.
- A Bayesian calibration and a Bayesian comparison of variants suit a small test set. They say how sure we are, not only which number is bigger.

## Where the ideas came from
- **Indy Johar's wiki.** The climate-to-violence cascade register was audited in July 2026. The audit found it had left out how often shocks fizzle. Its lesson: a model that runs towards the dramatic outcome "has encoded the wrong prior". This shapes step 3: the burden of proof is on moving.
- **Prateek's cascade modelling.** Going from the press release to the original paper corrected 15 of 28 figures. This is parked as a primary-source check, to be tested.
- **The xCO Assumption Chain Map.** This is parked as the keystone assumption check, to be tested.
- **Prateek, 13 November 2026:** consider Bayesian methods. This gave the odds-scale pooling, the Bayesian calibration, the Bayesian variant comparison and the `v2-bayes-update` variant.

## How we test it
1. Build a test set of a few hundred resolved questions. Limit news searches to before each question opened.
2. Run `v0-template` and `v1-spine` on it in dry-run mode. Nothing is posted.
3. Compare Brier score overall and by question type, as a Bayesian estimate of how likely v1 is to be better. Report the cost per question.
4. Say plainly when the sample is too small to tell.

**Lookahead risk:** some news or market data may reflect what happened after a question opened. The report will say how far this was ruled out.
